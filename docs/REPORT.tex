\section{Framing and contribution}

Human--AI decision pipelines are usually evaluated \emph{within} a paradigm: a learning-to-defer
(L2D) method against other L2D methods, or a confirmation/consultation rule against a single-human
or AI-alone baseline. The \textbf{Hybrid Confirmation Tree (HCT)} of Berger et al.\ (HCT-1,
\arxiv{2602.02375}; follow-up HCT-2, \arxiv{2603.29866}) and the L2D line of Okati, De \&
G\'omez-Rodr\'iguez (NeurIPS 2021, \arxiv{2103.08902}) and Mozannar \& Sontag (ICML 2020,
\arxiv{2006.01862}) have, to our knowledge, never been compared \textbf{head-to-head on a shared
dataset, backbone, and evaluation split}. That comparison --- not the Pareto plot, which is only its
visualization --- is the contribution here. We adopt the cost--accuracy trade-off framing of De Toni
et al.\ (NeurIPS 2024, framing only; not implemented).

The result is a \textbf{null}: on the Galaxy Zoo binary task, with a shared strong AI backbone,
\emph{no deployable human-in-the-loop arm significantly beats AI-alone}. We report this honestly and
explain why, rather than dressing up an overlapping confidence interval as a win.

\section{Dataset and the non-expert-rater caveat}

We use the \textbf{Galaxy Zoo binary 10k subset} (spiral vs.\ early-type/smooth) that Okati 2021
uses (their footnotes 8--9), sourced from the Kaggle Galaxy Zoo Challenge aggregate vote fractions.

HCT needs \textbf{real per-rater labels} (to draw two humans $h_1$, $h_2$ from a per-image urn),
which Okati's repository does not ship. We recover them by matching each Kaggle galaxy to the
Willett et al.\ 2013 GZ2 catalog via a content-based vote-signature crosswalk
(\texttt{docs/HANDOFF.md} \S5): \textbf{5{,}904 of 10{,}000 images (59\%) match} at high precision;
after dropping \texttt{star\_or\_artifact}-mode galaxies and images with too few votes for a
without-replacement draw, \textbf{$N = 4{,}621$} survive (train 3{,}234 / val 693 / \textbf{test
694}; \texttt{data/split\_manifest.json}). The matched subset is morphologically representative
(binary-hardness 0.52 vs.\ 0.48 overall), so the 41\% drop is not a difficulty-selected bias --- but
\textbf{59\% coverage is a disclosed limitation}, not full coverage.

\textbf{The raters are Galaxy Zoo citizen-science volunteers, not domain experts} (Okati's ``human
experts'' is loose terminology; \texttt{HANDOFF.md} \S2). This matters concretely: (i) the
single-human \emph{accuracy ceiling} is the volunteer crowd's, not an astronomer's; (ii) HCT's
tie-breaking second draw $h_2$ comes from the same volunteer vote distribution, so on genuinely
ambiguous galaxies it is right only about as often as a coin flip. Both depress the headroom
available to any human-querying arm. Crucially, the ``ground truth'' $y$ is itself the
\textbf{crowd-consensus majority}, so every accuracy below is \emph{agreement with consensus, not
with objective truth} (\S6).

\section{Methods and the invariants that make the comparison fair}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AI-alone} --- the shared backbone thresholded at 0.5 (cost 0).
  \item \textbf{Single-human} --- one urn draw per image (cost 1).
  \item \textbf{HCT} (HCT-1, Fig.~1): the human $h_1$ and the binarized AI decide independently; if
    they \textbf{agree}, accept (cost 1); if they \textbf{disagree}, draw $h_2$ and take its call
    (cost 2). Expected human cost $= 1 + P(\text{human--AI disagree})$, \textbf{bounded in $[1,2]$},
    swept via the AI binarization threshold $\theta$ --- the \emph{only} HCT knob. HCT is a decision
    rule, not a trained model.
  \item \textbf{L2D-Okati} (Thm.~3): for a fixed classifier, the optimal triage thresholds the
    per-instance gap between expected machine and human loss; a budget $b$ caps the deferral
    fraction. Cost $=$ deferral fraction $\in [0,1]$.
  \item \textbf{L2D-Mozannar} (eq.~10): the consistent convex surrogate $L_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\alpha}$;
    the deferral knob $\alpha$ sweeps a smooth curve. Cost $=$ deferral fraction $\in [0,1]$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Shared-backbone + frozen-split invariants}
(\texttt{GROUND\_TRUTH.md} \S7; \texttt{HANDOFF.md} \S10): every arm consumes the \emph{same}
committed backbone scores/embeddings and the \emph{same} seed${=}0$ 70/15/15 split (hash guarded by
\texttt{make verify-split}); one shared metrics module computes all numbers.

\paragraph{Deviation --- Option B}
(logged, \texttt{docs/DECISIONS.md}). The L2D papers \emph{co-train} classifier and rejector. We
instead \textbf{freeze the shared backbone and learn only the rejector/triage} on top of its
embeddings. This is deliberate: co-training would give each L2D arm a \emph{different} classifier,
confounding the comparison with backbone differences. Freezing makes the AI identical across all
arms, so any measured gap is attributable to the deferral policy alone --- it \textbf{removes a
confound rather than adding one}. The cost is that we measure these L2D methods' \emph{triage}
quality, not their end-to-end co-training; we do not import their co-trained accuracy claims here.

\paragraph{Curve-shape and cost-regime asymmetry (stated explicitly, never obscured).}
HCT is a \textbf{coarse marker locus}, drawn as discrete markers --- sweeping $\theta$ moves its
expected cost only within a narrow band (observed 1.18--1.23 here) because cost is
$1 + P(\text{disagree})$ and is structurally \textbf{bounded in $[1,2]$}. The L2D arms are
\textbf{smooth lines} whose cost ranges over \textbf{$[0,1]$}. The two methods therefore live in
\textbf{disjoint cost regimes} (L2D in $[0,1]$, HCT in $[1,2]$) and are \emph{not} mutually
Pareto-comparable point-for-point; HCT is never drawn as a continuous curve. This is a genuine
property of the methods, not an artefact of plotting.

\paragraph{Reading the arms.}
L2D-Okati(learned) peaks at 0.834 @ cost 0.10 then declines as more (often correct) machine
decisions are handed to noisier humans; Mozannar's best deferral point (0.822) sits \emph{below}
AI-alone in the mean (its CI still overlaps) and degrades further with deferral. HCT's accuracy is
essentially flat ($\sim$0.81) across $\theta$ and sits below AI-alone in the mean. The Okati
\textbf{oracle} (dashed) --- which peeks at test labels to triage perfectly --- climbs to 0.892, but
it is a \textbf{non-deployable upper bound} and is excluded from the deployable frontier.

\section{Central finding (an honest null)}

\textbf{No deployable arm significantly beats AI-alone.} The verdict is computed mechanically in
eval (\texttt{any\_deployable\_\allowbreak arm\_beats\_\allowbreak ai\_alone = false}, figure
manifest): every deployable
arm's CI either \textbf{overlaps} AI-alone (L2D-Okati-learned, HCT, Mozannar) or sits
\textbf{strictly below} it (single-human is the only arm whose CI excludes 0.829 --- it is
significantly \emph{worse}). L2D-Okati-learned's $+0.5$\,pt mean at cost 0.10 is within noise; HCT's
mean is below AI-alone at cost $>1$.

Interpretation: \textbf{on consensus-labeled Galaxy Zoo with a strong AI, human deferral buys
auditability, not accuracy.} HCT's value is that \emph{at least one human always approves the
deployed decision} at a bounded, transparent, training-free cost --- a governance property, not an
accuracy gain. The oracle shows $\sim$6\,pts of headroom \emph{exists} in principle, but it is
\textbf{unrealizable} with a deployable rejector on these frozen embeddings: the learned triage
cannot identify which machine errors a volunteer would fix.

\paragraph{Upstream self-claims are not findings here.}
HCT-1 reports 28--44\% cost savings \emph{on its own datasets}; that is a property of those
datasets, \textbf{not} a result on Galaxy Zoo, and we do not import it. Likewise we measure the L2D
arms' triage over a frozen backbone (Option B), not their co-trained end-to-end numbers. Every claim
in this report traces to an artifact under \texttt{results/}.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single dataset, single backbone.} One task (Galaxy Zoo binary), one frozen
    ResNet-50. No claim generalizes beyond this setting.
  \item \textbf{Accuracy $=$ agreement with crowd consensus, not truth} (\texttt{HANDOFF.md} \S9).
    Because $y$ is the volunteer majority, a single human's accuracy is the majority vote share by
    construction, and HCT's $h_2$ is $\sim$50\% on near-split galaxies by construction --- this
    compresses inter-arm gaps and is a \emph{threat to the comparison itself}, not a footnote.
  \item \textbf{Non-expert raters} (\S2): the human ceiling and the $h_2$ tiebreak are
    volunteer-quality.
  \item \textbf{59\% crosswalk coverage} (\S2): 4{,}621 of 10{,}000; disclosed, representative, but
    not complete.
  \item \textbf{Urn structural limits} (\texttt{HANDOFF.md} \S6): $h_1 \perp h_2 \mid x$ is enforced
    (both are independent draws from the same per-image distribution), so \textbf{$h_2$ is a second
    \emph{draw}, not a second \emph{human}}; the positive human--human correlation Berger studies
    lives only in between-image urn-composition variance, and the opinion-leader ($\rho$)
    reconstruction is \textbf{not supported} by aggregate counts.
  \item \textbf{Option-B deviation} (\S3): triage-only, not the papers' co-training; removes a
    backbone confound but does not evaluate end-to-end co-training.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility boundary} (\texttt{docs/REPRODUCE.md}): \texttt{make all} regenerates
    this report's figure and table deterministically on CPU from the committed frozen artifacts; the
    backbone training + embedding export (GPU/Colab, \texttt{notebooks/m2\_colab.ipynb}) and the
    raw-catalog label build are upstream of that interface and are not re-run by \texttt{make all}.
\end{itemize}
